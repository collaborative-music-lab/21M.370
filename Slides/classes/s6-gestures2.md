## Performance Practice

What are we engaged in when performing electronic music?

1. Levels of engagement when performing
	1. Signal Level: Direct control of sound events
	2. Event Level: scheduling and arranging sonic events
	3. Composition Level: evolution of sonic events over time

2. Temporal connection between gesture and sound
	- How tight is the feedback loop?
	- Can we zoom out to think about structure?

3. Automation and outsourcing: What can we choose to not control?
	- time: quantization, sequencing
	- harmony: chord progressions, sections
	- sound: presets, predefined mappings
	- accompaniment: virtual drummers, arpeggiators, delay/reverb

---

## Signal: direct control of sound

If playing an acoustic instrument is "touching physics", what is signal level control of a digital instrument?

- sound stops when you stop playing
- excitation gestures: putting energy into a system
	- impulses, shaking, bowing
	- awareness of smoothing, especially for decays: how long does it take for energy to dissipate?
- modulation gestures: changing parameters over time
	- "manual lfo": vibrato, tremolo
	- sound sculpting: exploration of timbre space
- use psychoacoustic parameter coupling: loud impulses excite higher frequencies
- tight timing / low latency is very helpful

Examples: continuum and other keyboard-adjacent instruments, trautonium

---

## Sign: Event level control of sound

Letting go of signal level control allows us to focus on combinations and sequences of events

- excitation: not triggered manually, but by a process such as a timed algorithm (sequencer, arpeggiator)
- modulation: actual LFO control, envelopes(?), sequenced parameter changes
- letting of manual control allows for access to a larger parameter space

Examples: monome grid, makenoise 0-ctrl

---

## Symbol: Compositional level control of sound

Making larger-level decisions of musical structure without manually determining events

- The classic example is a DJ, using records as base material to shape a musical form
- combinations of preset sequences or musical structures
- larger-scale algorithmic determination of musical patterns
- might be tied to semantic concepts translated into patterns, e.g. euclidean rhythms

---

## Implications of Signal / Sign / Symbol

It is common to combine elements from different perspectives in a performance.

- Different levels afford different musical thought processes in performance
- Different levels *suggest* different amounts of preparation, practice, pedagogy
- Preconceived expectations of the desired musical experience will often inform instrument design. 
	- Attempts to recreate traditional musical performance will suggest signal level control
	- Dance music will often suggest sign level control
	- Attempts to recreate recorded music, or expectations for specific sound quality will often suggest symbol level

Overall, focus on what you want to be **thinking** while you are performing. . .
